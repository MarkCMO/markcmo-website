Marketing Glossary

What Is LinkedIn Marketing?

LinkedIn marketing is the use of LinkedIn's organic and paid tools to build professional brand presence, generate B2B leads, and engage decision-makers within target accounts and industries. LinkedIn has over 1 billion members worldwide, with 65 million decision-makers and 10 million C-suite executives actively using the platform -- making it the highest-concentration professional audience of any social network. For B2B companies, LinkedIn marketing spans four interconnected areas: executive thought leadership (personal brand content from founders and executives), company page strategy, LinkedIn Ads (sponsored content, lead gen forms, message ads), and social selling (direct relationship-building and outreach by the sales team).

How LinkedIn Marketing Works for B2B

LinkedIn marketing is most effective when the company's executives and founders are publishing substantive, opinion-driven content consistently -- not when the company page is posting product announcements and corporate news. LinkedIn's algorithm significantly favors personal profiles over company pages, and it favors content that generates meaningful comments over content that generates only reactions. A founder or CMO posting 3 times per week on LinkedIn with specific, data-backed insights about their industry will consistently outperform a company page posting daily generic content.

LinkedIn Ads provide the most precise B2B audience targeting available in paid media -- enabling advertisers to target by job title, seniority, company size, industry, LinkedIn group membership, and skills. This precision makes LinkedIn CPCs higher than other platforms ($5 to $15 on average) but the audience quality for B2B lead generation typically justifies the premium. LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms -- which pre-populate with profile data -- consistently convert at 2 to 3 times the rate of landing page-based campaigns for B2B audiences.

LinkedIn is the only social platform where you can put a specific message in front of a VP of Marketing at a 500-person SaaS company in Austin -- and that precision is worth paying a premium for.

Core Components of LinkedIn Marketing

  • Executive Thought LeadershipBuilding the personal LinkedIn presence of founders, executives, and subject matter experts -- with a content strategy, publishing cadence, and ghostwriting or editorial support that consistently produces high-engagement, insight-driven content.
  • Company Page OptimizationOptimizing the LinkedIn company page with complete profile information, keyword-rich description, consistent posting, employee advocacy activation, and product pages -- building the company's professional presence that reinforces individual thought leadership.
  • LinkedIn Ads and Lead GenerationManaging LinkedIn paid campaigns: sponsored content (single image, carousel, video), LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms, message ads, and conversation ads -- with precise audience targeting and continuous creative testing.
  • Social Selling and Sales NavigatorEnabling sales teams to use LinkedIn Sales Navigator for account research, relationship mapping, warm outreach, and engagement with prospect content -- building pipeline through relationship-first rather than cold-pitch approaches.
  • LinkedIn Analytics and Social Selling IndexTracking LinkedIn performance: organic reach, engagement rate, follower growth, ad performance, and the Social Selling Index (SSI) -- a LinkedIn-provided score measuring profile strength, relationship building, and engagement quality.
  • Content Calendar and ProductionDeveloping and maintaining a LinkedIn content calendar that aligns executive and company content around consistent themes, industry events, and campaign priorities -- ensuring regular, strategic publishing rather than ad-hoc posting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is LinkedIn marketing?

LinkedIn marketing is the use of LinkedIn to build professional brand presence, generate B2B leads, and engage decision-makers. It includes organic strategies (executive thought leadership, company page content, employee advocacy) and paid strategies (sponsored content, LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms, message ads). LinkedIn is the most important social media marketing platform for B2B companies because of its professional audience concentration and precise targeting capabilities.

How do you generate leads on LinkedIn?

The most effective LinkedIn lead generation approaches are: (1) LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms in paid campaigns -- these pre-populate with profile data and convert at 2 to 3 times the rate of landing page campaigns; (2) Organic thought leadership that generates inbound connection requests and DMs from interested prospects; (3) Social selling outreach from the sales team -- engaging with prospect content, sending personalized connection requests, and following up with relevant resources; (4) LinkedIn Events and webinars that attract registrations from the target audience.

How much do LinkedIn Ads cost?

LinkedIn Ads have average CPCs of $5 to $15 and CPMs of $30 to $80 -- significantly higher than Meta or Google Display. LinkedIn Lead Gen Form CPLs typically range from $50 to $200 depending on offer quality and audience specificity. Despite higher unit costs, LinkedIn's B2B audience targeting precision typically produces higher-quality leads with higher close rates than lower-cost platforms -- making LinkedIn ROI competitive on a cost-per-qualified-opportunity basis.

What is the LinkedIn Social Selling Index?

The LinkedIn Social Selling Index (SSI) is a 0-to-100 score provided by LinkedIn that measures four dimensions of social selling effectiveness: establishing professional brand (profile completeness and content publishing), finding the right people (search and research behavior), engaging with insights (content engagement quality), and building relationships (connection building and messaging). A higher SSI correlates with increased profile visibility, organic reach, and InMail response rates.

How often should B2B executives post on LinkedIn?

B2B executives should post 3 to 5 times per week on LinkedIn for maximum organic reach and audience growth. Each post should be substantive -- sharing a specific insight, data point, or opinion rather than generic motivational content or reposts. Consistency matters more than frequency: posting 3 times per week every week for 12 months produces significantly more compounding reach than posting daily for 3 months and stopping.
