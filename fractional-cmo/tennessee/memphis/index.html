Memphis' Business Landscape

Memphis anchors the Mid-South regional economy with a GDP exceeding $75 billion. The city's logistics infrastructure - FedEx World Hub, the Memphis International Airport (the busiest cargo airport in the Western Hemisphere), and the Port of Memphis - creates a commercial ecosystem unlike any other in the country. Around this core, healthcare, food processing, and professional services have built a diversified economy of considerable scale.

Logistics and Supply Chain

FedEx, Nike's North American distribution hub, and hundreds of third-party logistics companies make Memphis the beating heart of American supply chain. Vendors serving this sector - from warehouse management software to staffing agencies to fleet services - compete for relationships with procurement teams that prioritize reliability, compliance, and cost-per-unit above all else.

Healthcare and Medical Research

St. Jude Children's Research Hospital, Methodist Le Bonheur Healthcare, and Baptist Memorial Health Care anchor a world-class medical ecosystem in Memphis. The city's healthcare sector attracts researchers, clinicians, and medical device companies from across the country - creating a B2B market for health IT, clinical services, and pharmaceutical companies with highly sophisticated buyers.

Food Manufacturing and Agribusiness

Memphis is a major center for food processing and agribusiness, with companies like Dunavant Enterprises and numerous food manufacturing facilities operating in the region. The proximity to the Mississippi Delta's agricultural production creates a dense ecosystem of food brands, ingredient suppliers, and cold chain logistics companies that compete on both price and traceability.
