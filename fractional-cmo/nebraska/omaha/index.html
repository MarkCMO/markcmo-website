Omaha's Business Landscape

Omaha defies its modest national profile with an economic profile that rivals cities several times its size. The city's Fortune 500 headquarters density - Berkshire Hathaway, Union Pacific, Mutual of Omaha, Kiewit, ConAgra Brands - creates both a sophisticated buyer market and an extraordinary talent pool of executives who have operated at the highest levels of business. Financial services, insurance, transportation, food processing, and construction represent the core industries, but healthcare, technology, and professional services have expanded significantly. The city's comparatively low cost of living and business-friendly environment also make it a magnet for entrepreneurs and growth-stage companies who can access Midwest capital and talent without coastal overhead.

Financial Services and Insurance

Berkshire Hathaway's presence - and its dozens of wholly owned subsidiaries operating across insurance, reinsurance, and financial services - has made Omaha one of the most important financial services markets in the country. Mutual of Omaha, one of the nation's largest insurance and financial services companies, adds tens of thousands of employees and a massive client ecosystem to the mix. Companies in the financial services and insurance technology space have extraordinary opportunities in Omaha if they can demonstrate institutional quality and long-term value.

Transportation and Logistics

Union Pacific's Omaha headquarters anchors one of the largest transportation and logistics employer bases in the Midwest. The railroad's supplier ecosystem - technology vendors, engineering services, maintenance providers, and logistics software companies - represents a significant B2B market where precision, reliability, and long-term contract performance define competitive selection. Kiewit's construction and engineering operations add another layer of industrial services procurement activity to the transportation and infrastructure sector.

Food Processing and Healthcare

Companies serving either sector benefit from Omaha's Midwest operational culture - one that rewards dependability, value delivery, and long-term relationship investment over short-term transactional thinking.
