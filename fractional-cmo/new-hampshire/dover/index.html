Dover's Business Landscape

Dover combines the economic gravity of a Strafford County regional hub with the growth dynamics of New Hampshire's expanding seacoast economy. New Hampshire's fourth-largest city has evolved from its historic textile and manufacturing roots along the Cocheco River into a diversified economy anchored by healthcare, technology, education, and professional services. The absence of state income and sales taxes continues to attract businesses and professionals from Massachusetts and other high-tax states, driving population growth and commercial real estate development that is reshaping Dover's downtown and surrounding commercial corridors. Proximity to Portsmouth's established business community, defense contracting ecosystem, and hospitality economy adds further economic dynamism to the Dover market.

Healthcare and Wentworth-Douglass

Wentworth-Douglass Hospital is the economic centerpiece of Dover's healthcare sector - a full-service regional medical center that became a Mass General Brigham affiliate, connecting the Dover market to the clinical standards, research programs, and procurement frameworks of one of the world's leading academic health systems. The hospital employs thousands in Strafford County and generates substantial procurement activity for medical device companies, health IT vendors, clinical staffing firms, facilities management services, and healthcare professional services. As the hospital continues to expand its outpatient network and specialty care capacity, the vendor opportunity in Dover's healthcare market will grow. For healthcare-adjacent businesses, establishing credibility in a Mass General Brigham-affiliated procurement environment is a significant differentiator in competitive regional markets.

University of New Hampshire Proximity

The University of New Hampshire in neighboring Durham is one of New England's leading public research universities, with particular strengths in engineering, earth sciences, and ocean sciences that reflect the seacoast region's research priorities. UNH's proximity to Dover creates multiple economic connections: a large student and faculty population that supports Dover's retail and services economy, technology transfer and startup formation activity that feeds Dover's technology sector, and institutional procurement that Dover vendors with academic market credibility can access. UNH's Interoperability Laboratory in Durham is a particularly important tech commercialization hub, supporting networking and IoT technology companies with testing and standards certification services that attract technology vendors to the seacoast region.

Technology and Manufacturing

Dover's Cocheco River mill district has been repurposed from its historic textile manufacturing origins into a mixed commercial and technology workspace - a pattern of adaptive reuse that has created affordable commercial space for technology companies, creative agencies, and professional services firms. Manufacturing in Dover continues in precision electronics, specialty industrial production, and defense-adjacent components supply. The seacoast corridor's connection to Portsmouth Naval Shipyard and the broader defense contracting ecosystem of the Pease Tradeport creates demand for specialized technical services and advanced manufacturing. Technology companies serving the defense sector find Dover's proximity to Portsmouth and its connections to UNH's engineering programs strategically valuable for talent recruitment and research partnerships.
