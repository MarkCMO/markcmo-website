Gillette's Energy Economy

Gillette is the seat of Campbell County and the commercial center of the Powder River Basin - a geological formation that holds the largest coal reserves in the United States. Wyoming produces more coal than any other state, and the majority of that coal comes from mines within 50 miles of Gillette. Beyond coal, the basin produces significant volumes of oil and natural gas, supporting a multi-billion-dollar extraction economy that drives construction, logistics, equipment, professional services, and technology spending across the region. As data center operators and energy-intensive industries increasingly target Wyoming for its low-cost power, Gillette is also seeing new technology sector interest that adds diversity to the traditional extractive economy.

Coal and Energy Mining

The Powder River Basin's surface coal mines include some of the largest mining operations in the world by production volume. Major operators run continuous mining operations requiring enormous vendor ecosystems: dragline and shovel maintenance, haul truck fleets, conveyor systems, explosive services, environmental compliance, and workforce safety programs. Marketing to these operators requires deep familiarity with mining procurement processes, environmental regulatory frameworks, and the multi-year contract structures that govern major surface mining service agreements. Positioning for preferred vendor status in this environment is a marketing challenge as much as an operational one.

Oil and Gas Services

Campbell County's oil and gas production adds a second major revenue stream to Gillette's energy economy. Many Gillette companies serve both coal and oil and gas clients, requiring marketing that speaks credibly to both buyer communities simultaneously. The shale technology revolution has also brought newer completion service companies and data-driven production optimization vendors into the Gillette market, adding technology-oriented procurement alongside traditional oilfield services contracting.
