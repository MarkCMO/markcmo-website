What Does a Fractional CMO Do?

A fractional Chief Marketing Officer takes full strategic ownership of a company's marketing function without being a full-time employee. The role is not advisory and it is not consultative in the traditional sense. A fractional CMO sits in the executive seat. That means setting marketing strategy, building or managing the marketing team, owning the budget, and being accountable to revenue metrics the same way an in-house CMO would be.

The day-to-day work of a fractional CMO varies by company stage and need. For a $5M company that has never had marketing leadership, the first 90 days look like: auditing the current state, defining the ideal customer profile, building positioning and messaging from scratch, and launching the two or three demand generation channels most likely to generate pipeline fast. For a $30M company in transition between full-time CMOs, the work looks more like: stabilizing existing programs, retaining the marketing team, and running a proper search for the permanent hire while keeping revenue momentum going.

In every engagement, a fractional CMO is responsible for:

  • Go-to-market strategy and planning - defining how the company reaches, attracts, and converts its ideal customer
  • Demand generation - building the systems that create predictable, measurable pipeline across paid, organic, email, and outbound channels
  • Brand positioning and messaging - clarifying what the company stands for, who it serves, and why buyers should choose it over alternatives
  • Marketing team management - hiring, managing, and developing marketing staff or managing agency and contractor relationships
  • Martech stack oversight - selecting, configuring, and maintaining the marketing technology that makes the operation run
  • Revenue operations alignment - connecting marketing to sales so that every lead has a clear path to becoming a conversation and a close
  • Budget stewardship - allocating marketing spend toward the channels and programs with the best return
  • Board and investor reporting - communicating marketing performance and strategy in terms the leadership team and investors care about

Who Needs a Fractional CMO?

The fractional CMO model exists to solve a specific problem: mid-market and growth-stage companies need senior marketing leadership but cannot justify a $250,000-$400,000 full-time hire. That gap is real, and it stalls more companies than almost any other resource constraint.

You should consider hiring a fractional CMO if any of these situations apply to your business:

  • Revenue is between $1M and $50M and growth has plateaued
  • You have a marketing team but no senior leader giving them direction
  • You just lost your CMO or VP of Marketing and need to bridge the gap without a 6-month search
  • You are preparing for a fundraising round or M&A event and need marketing to reflect your actual value
  • You are launching into a new market or product line and need a market entry strategy
  • Your marketing agency keeps producing activity without producing revenue
  • You cannot connect marketing spend to pipeline or closed deals
  • Sales and marketing are not aligned and leads are falling through

When NOT to Hire a Fractional CMO

A fractional CMO is not the right fit for every company, and being direct about that upfront is something I consider a baseline of professional honesty.

If your business is under $500,000 in revenue, you likely do not yet have the infrastructure, team, or budget to extract full value from a fractional CMO engagement. At that stage, a growth-focused marketing manager or a focused agency relationship is probably a better fit for your needs and budget.

If you do not yet have product-market fit, no amount of marketing leadership will fix the underlying product or sales problem. Marketing amplifies what is already working. It cannot substitute for finding what works.

If what you actually need is someone executing tactics daily, that is a marketing manager role, not a fractional CMO role. A CMO sets strategy and manages execution. If your team is missing the person doing the execution work, that is a hiring problem, not a leadership problem.

"Most businesses don't have a marketing problem. They have a systems problem wearing a marketing mask. Before we talk campaigns, we figure out what is actually broken."

How a Fractional CMO Engagement Works

Every engagement follows a structured process designed to deliver real results, not just strategy documents. Here is how it works from first call to ongoing execution.

1

Free 30-Minute Diagnostic Call

The first conversation is free and focused entirely on your situation. No pitch, no slide deck. We talk about where you are, where you are trying to go, what has been tried, and what has not worked. By the end of the call you will have a clear picture of whether a fractional CMO engagement makes sense for your business right now.

2

Marketing Diagnostic Audit (Week 1-2)

The first deliverable of every engagement is a full marketing audit: channels, campaigns, team, tech stack, positioning, and competitive landscape. This is where most of the real insight lives. The audit identifies what is working, what is broken, and what is missing entirely. It becomes the foundation for the strategy that follows.

3

90-Day Revenue Roadmap (Week 2-3)

Based on the audit findings, a prioritized 90-day plan that tells the team exactly what to build, what to stop, and what to accelerate. The roadmap is tied directly to revenue targets, not marketing vanity metrics. Every item on it has an owner, a deadline, and a clear connection to pipeline or revenue.

4

Execution, Management, and Accountability (Ongoing)

The fractional CMO role is active, not advisory. That means attending leadership team meetings, managing or mentoring marketing staff, working directly with agencies and contractors, and reviewing everything that goes out the door with an eye toward revenue impact. Weekly reporting connects every activity to pipeline and revenue outcomes.

Fractional CMO - Frequently Asked Questions

What is a fractional CMO?
A fractional CMO is a senior Chief Marketing Officer who works with a company on a part-time or retainer basis rather than as a full-time employee. The fractional CMO takes full ownership of marketing strategy and execution, manages the team, and is accountable to revenue outcomes - the same responsibilities as an in-house CMO, at a fraction of the cost. Retainer engagements typically range from $8,000 to $20,000 per month.
How much does a fractional CMO cost?
A fractional CMO typically costs between $8,000 and $20,000 per month on a retainer basis. Shorter sprint engagements for specific deliverables start at $8,000 for a 4-to-6-week period. Compare this to a full-time CMO hire, which averages $280,000 to $400,000 per year in salary alone, plus benefits, equity, and a recruiting timeline of 4 to 6 months. Mark Gabrielli's engagements are month-to-month with no long-term contract required.
What is the difference between a fractional CMO and a marketing agency?
A marketing agency executes tactics within a defined scope and is accountable to deliverables. A fractional CMO owns strategy, integrates across the entire business, manages the team and agency relationships, sets budget priorities, and is accountable to business outcomes such as pipeline and revenue. Agencies report to a CMO. A fractional CMO reports directly to the CEO and sits inside the leadership team.
What is the difference between a fractional CMO and an interim CMO?
An interim CMO is typically a full-time, temporary placement hired to bridge the gap between permanent CMO hires. A fractional CMO is part-time by design and often serves multiple companies simultaneously. Interim engagements are usually 3 to 6 months at full-time rates. Fractional engagements can run indefinitely as the right long-term model for companies that do not need or cannot afford a full-time hire.
How many hours per week does a fractional CMO work?
The hours vary by engagement scope and retainer level, but most fractional CMO engagements run between 10 and 20 hours per week. At the lower end, this covers strategic direction, weekly leadership team meetings, and oversight of ongoing programs. At the higher end, it includes hands-on execution, team management, and active campaign work. The goal is to do the work that requires CMO-level judgment, not to fill a timesheet.
How quickly can a fractional CMO start?
A fractional CMO can typically start within days to two weeks of engagement agreement. This is one of the clearest advantages over a full-time hire, which averages 4 to 6 months from search launch to start date. The marketing audit and first deliverables are usually complete within the first two to three weeks of engagement.
How long does a fractional CMO engagement last?
Most engagements run between 6 and 18 months. The first 90 days are the most intensive, covering the audit, strategy, and infrastructure build. After that, many companies continue at a reduced retainer level with the fractional CMO moving into a more advisory and oversight capacity. Engagements are month-to-month - there is no long-term contract that locks you in.
What size company needs a fractional CMO?
The fractional CMO model is best suited for companies generating between $1 million and $50 million in annual revenue. This is the stage where marketing leadership has a direct and measurable impact on growth, but a $300,000+ full-time CMO hire is not yet justified. Companies above $50M often find it makes sense to hire a full-time CMO, sometimes with the fractional CMO's help finding and onboarding the right person.
Can a fractional CMO work remotely?
Yes. Most fractional CMO work is conducted remotely via video meetings, collaborative tools, and embedded communication with your team. Mark Gabrielli serves clients across all 50 states remotely, with in-person availability for key strategy sessions, board meetings, and critical milestones in certain markets.
Will a fractional CMO manage our marketing team?
Yes. Team management is a core part of the fractional CMO role, not an add-on. This includes managing direct reports, setting performance expectations, providing feedback, and building the team structure that can scale with the company. If there is no team yet, the fractional CMO helps design the hiring plan and evaluate candidates.
Can a fractional CMO help with fundraising?
Yes. Building the marketing narrative for a fundraising round is one of the highest-leverage things a fractional CMO can do. This includes the investor deck story, the market size framing, the go-to-market slide, the unit economics narrative, and the board-level reporting cadence that demonstrates marketing-sourced revenue. Investors evaluate marketing leadership closely. Having a credible fractional CMO in the room significantly improves that signal.
What results can I expect in the first 90 days?
In the first 90 days, a fractional CMO engagement typically delivers: a completed marketing audit identifying the top 3 to 5 constraints, a documented positioning and messaging framework, a 90-day revenue roadmap with prioritized actions, and 1 to 2 demand generation programs launched and generating early data. Some companies see pipeline movement in the first 30 days. Most see measurable lead flow improvement by day 60 to 90. The goal is not to have every campaign perfect by day 90. The goal is to have the system in place so everything that comes after compounds.
Do I need a fractional CMO or a marketing manager?
If your problem is that nobody is setting marketing strategy and making decisions at the executive level, you need a fractional CMO. If your strategy is clear and your problem is execution bandwidth, you need a marketing manager. Many companies need both. A common pattern is hiring a fractional CMO to set the strategy and build the system, then hiring a marketing manager to run it, with the fractional CMO transitioning into an advisory role.
