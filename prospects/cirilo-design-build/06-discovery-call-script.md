## Call structure (60 minutes)

### Block 1: Rapport + frame (5 min)

Open warm, founder-to-founder. Mark is a fellow entrepreneur, not a vendor.

> "Ramon, Tiffany, thanks for the time. Before we get into the work I sent you, I want to learn a bit about you both. How did you decide to start Cirilo? What's the origin story?"

Listen. Take notes. Look for:
- Year founded (the audit flagged "six years ago" with no date)
- How many pools built to date
- What share of revenue is pool vs reno vs additions today
- The "why" behind the design-build positioning

### Block 2: Business state (15 min)

Ask in this order:

1. **Revenue.** "What did Cirilo do in revenue last year? What's the goal for this year?"
2. **Pipeline today.** "How many active projects do you have right now? How many at proposal stage? How many in pre-design?"
3. **Lead source mix.** "Where are your current leads coming from? Word-of-mouth, past client, referral, web, Houzz?"
4. **Close rate.** "Of the consultations Tiffany books, what percentage become signed contracts?"
5. **Ticket.** "What's a typical pool build for you, low end to high end? Average?"
6. **Capacity.** "How many pool projects can your current crew handle simultaneously? What's the max per year before you need to hire?"
7. **Marketing spend today.** "What are you spending on marketing right now, anything? Houzz, SEO, ads, anything?"
8. **Owner time on marketing.** "How much of Tiffany's week goes to marketing-adjacent work today, vs operations and client?"

### Block 3: The audit findings (10 min)

> "Did you get a chance to look at the discovery audit I sent?"

If yes: "Anything jump out, anything surprise you, anything you disagreed with?"

If no: walk them through the 4-page scorecard and the 15 red flags. Don't rub their nose in it. Frame as opportunity: "Every one of these is fixable in 90 days. Most are free. The reason I sent this is so you can see the gap with your own eyes, not because I'm trying to sell you on a problem."

Hit these three landmines specifically:
- "Zero Google reviews" - frame as "the single biggest barrier to your next paid dollar converting"
- "910 area code" - frame as "the easiest free win, I can port a Charlotte number this week"
- "No Google Business Profile" - frame as "60% of your future customers start on Google Maps and you don't show up"

### Block 4: The strategic frame (8 min)

> "Here's the position the audit pointed me to. Charlotte's luxury pool market has two camps. The big-brand volume guys (Anthony & Sylvan, Blue Haven, Premier) win on ad spend and read commodity. The credentialed boutique guys (Lombardo, Aloha, Tidal) win on prestige but are invisible online. Nobody owns 'design-led modern luxury with a modern marketing presence.' Your brand wrapper is already aimed at that seat. The work is to claim it before someone else notices."

Pause. Let it land.

> "The 90-day plan is straightforward. Fix the trust scaffolding. Turn on the channels the boutique tier ignores. Open a referral program nobody in the market is running. The 12-month outcome targets 22 to 32 signed pool contracts, which at your AOV puts you in the $5M to $7M revenue range from this program alone."

### Block 5: Pricing + tier walk (10 min)

> "I sent three tiers in the proposal. The honest recommendation is Tier 2 (Growth). Here's why."

- Tier 1 funds the trust fix and gets you ranking, but doesn't fund the partnership program or the content engine. Returns flatten at Month 6.
- Tier 3 is genuine category dominance in 9 months but only makes sense if you have the capital and the crew capacity to swallow 35 to 50 signed contracts in Year 1.
- Tier 2 lands at $487K Year 1, breaks even at 27 signed contracts, realistic outcome is 22 to 32. The trust fix ships in 90 days. The partnership program activates Month 3. The content engine compounds Month 4.

Then: **stop talking and listen for the objection.**

### Block 6: Objection handling (8 min)

Pre-loaded responses for the four likely objections:

**"$487K is more than we expected."**
> "I hear you. Tier 2 is the recommendation, not the requirement. Tier 1 at $230K is the lower-risk entry. If you start at Tier 1 and we're hitting the trust-stack outcomes at Day 90, you can upgrade to Tier 2 at the Day-90 review without penalty. The thing I do not recommend is starting at a lower number that doesn't fund the partnership program or the content layer, because those are the channels that compound."

**"How fast do we see leads?"**
> "GBP and LSA can produce inbound leads inside 30 to 45 days once the reviews and credentials are in place. Google Search produces inside 45 to 90 days. Partnership referrals start showing up in Month 3 when the first 5 partners are signed. Realistic first signed contract attributable to this program: Month 3 to Month 4. Realistic monthly signed-contract velocity at Month 6: 2 to 3 per month. At Month 12: 3 to 5 per month."

**"Why can't we just hire a Charlotte ad agency for $5K a month?"**
> "You could. Two reasons it won't work. First, no Charlotte agency runs a fractional CMO function. They'll run your Google Ads but won't build the partnership program, won't pitch press, won't fix the trust stack, won't structure the credentialing path, won't sit in your QBR. Second, an ad agency throwing $5K of media at a brand with zero reviews and no GBP burns the budget. The trust scaffolding has to come first, and that's not what an ad agency builds. The Tier 1 systems build at $38K is what gets you to the point where paid spend actually converts."

**"We need to think about it / talk to our accountant."**
> "Totally fair. Two asks: first, can we put a signature date on the calendar, even if it slips? Like 'we'll have an answer by next Friday.' Second, what's the one thing I can answer right now that would make the decision easier or harder?"

### Block 7: Close (4 min)

> "Here's what I propose. I'll send the proposal in DocuSign within the hour, Tier 2 selected by default. You and Ramon talk it through tonight. If Tier 2 is right, sign and send the 50% systems-build deposit. We kick off Day 0 on Monday. If you want to downgrade to Tier 1, change the tier in the doc before signing. If you want to think longer, we book a 30-minute follow-up for [Friday] and you tell me what's in the way. Sound right?"

Get one of three commitments:
1. **Sign within 48 hours** (ideal)
2. **Specific follow-up date locked on calendar** (acceptable)
3. **A "no" with a reason** (acceptable, frees Mark's calendar)

What is NOT acceptable: "we'll let you know" with no date. Push for the date.

## Qualification disqualifiers (when to walk away)

Walk away from the engagement if any of these is true:

- Revenue below $1.5M trailing twelve months AND no investor capital. The Tier 1 monthly retainer alone is 7% of a $1.5M business, which is too much load.
- Cannot point to 10+ past completed pool projects. Cirilo needs a portfolio reshoot to work and credibility on calls.
- NC GC license is not current or active.
- Owners are in conflict on the direction. (Watch for Ramon and Tiffany disagreeing on call.)
- They want to micromanage daily tactics. (Fractional CMO requires they trust the operator.)
- They want a "performance-only" deal with no retainer. (Mark doesn't take pure-performance engagements; foundation work has no clean attribution.)
