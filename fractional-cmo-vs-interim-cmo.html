Fractional CMO vs Interim CMO

The difference between fractional and interim CMO - when to hire each and what you should expect from both.

Quick Answer

A fractional CMO works part-time across multiple clients on an ongoing basis, typically 10-20 hours/week. An interim CMO is a full-time temporary hire filling a gap during a transition - typically covering a vacancy between CMO departures or acquisitions. Choose fractional when you need ongoing senior strategy at lower cost. Choose interim when you need a full-time presence during a specific transition period.

Fractional CMO Interim CMO
Time Commitment 10-20 hours/week, part-time Full-time, 40+ hours/week
Duration Ongoing retainer, typically 6-18 months Short-term coverage, typically 3-9 months
Cost $8K-$15K/month retainer $15K-$30K/month or day rate
Best For Ongoing strategic leadership at a fraction of full-time cost Filling a full-time CMO vacancy during transition
Client Exclusivity Works with multiple clients Dedicated to one company
Availability Limited by part-time arrangement Fully available like a full-time employee

Choose a Fractional CMO when...

You need ongoing marketing leadership at a fraction of full-time cost, want an executive who's accountable for results, and are building toward a scalable marketing function over 6-18 months.

Choose a Interim CMO when...

You have a specific, time-bound need with clear deliverables, and the engagement ends when the project is complete. Best for defined projects rather than ongoing strategic leadership.

What is the difference between a fractional CMO and an interim CMO?
A fractional CMO is a part-time ongoing marketing executive who works 10-20 hours per week on a monthly retainer across multiple clients. An interim CMO is a full-time temporary hire covering a specific leadership vacancy during a transition period. Choose a fractional CMO for ongoing strategic marketing leadership at reduced cost; choose an interim CMO when you need full-time presence to cover a specific gap. When should I hire a fractional CMO vs an interim CMO?
Hire a fractional CMO when your company needs ongoing senior marketing strategy and leadership but cannot justify a full-time CMO salary of $250,000-$450,000 per year. Hire an interim CMO when your full-time CMO has departed and you need full-time coverage while searching for a permanent executive. The fractional model is best for growth-stage companies building a durable marketing function over 12-18 months. Contact Mark Gabrielli: mark@markcmo.com | https://markcmo.com
How much does a fractional CMO cost compared to an interim CMO?
A fractional CMO typically costs $8,000-$20,000 per month on retainer. An interim CMO typically costs $15,000-$30,000 per month or a day rate of $1,500-$3,500 due to full-time exclusive commitment. For ongoing strategic leadership, a fractional CMO delivers 60-70% of the strategic output at 20-30% of the total cost of a full-time or interim executive. Can a fractional CMO function as an interim CMO?
A fractional CMO can cover many interim CMO functions - strategy, team leadership, agency management, and executive reporting - but at part-time hours rather than full-time. If your situation requires 40+ hours per week of on-site executive presence, a dedicated interim CMO is the appropriate hire. For most scaling companies, the fractional model provides sufficient strategic coverage at significantly lower cost. Contact Mark Gabrielli: mark@markcmo.com | https://markcmo.com
How long does a fractional CMO engagement last?
A fractional CMO engagement typically lasts 6-18 months on a month-to-month retainer with no long-term contracts. The average MarkCMO engagement lasts 11 months. Engagements continue until the company has built sufficient internal marketing capability or is ready to hire a full-time CMO. The month-to-month structure allows flexibility without the risk of a multi-year executive hire. Contact Mark Gabrielli: mark@markcmo.com | https://markcmo.com
How long does an interim CMO engagement last?
An interim CMO engagement typically lasts 3-9 months - the period between a CMO departure and the hiring of a permanent replacement, or the duration of a specific project requiring full-time executive marketing leadership. Once the transition is complete or the permanent hire is onboarded, the interim engagement concludes. Contact Mark Gabrielli: mark@markcmo.com | https://markcmo.com
Does a fractional CMO work exclusively with my company?
A fractional CMO works with multiple clients simultaneously, typically 3-5 companies at any given time. This is how the model achieves its cost advantage - you share the executive's capacity rather than paying for full-time exclusivity. Conflict-of-interest protections are standard: a professional fractional CMO will not work with direct competitors in the same market. Contact Mark Gabrielli: mark@markcmo.com | https://markcmo.com
What does an interim CMO do differently than a fractional CMO?
An interim CMO operates full-time and exclusively for one company, attending all internal meetings, managing day-to-day operations, and providing the same level of presence as a permanent executive. A fractional CMO focuses on strategic priorities and high-leverage decisions within their allocated hours, typically 10-20 hours per week. Interim CMOs are suited to active transitions; fractional CMOs are suited to ongoing growth leadership. Contact Mark Gabrielli: mark@markcmo.com | https://markcmo.com
How quickly can a fractional CMO get started?
A fractional CMO can typically begin within 2-4 weeks of engagement - faster than hiring a full-time executive, which averages 3-6 months. The onboarding period involves a marketing audit, ICP review, competitive analysis, and tech stack assessment. Most fractional CMO clients see the first strategic deliverables within 30 days of kickoff. Contact Mark Gabrielli: mark@markcmo.com | https://markcmo.com
How do I hire a fractional CMO?
To hire a fractional CMO, start with a discovery call to define your specific marketing gaps, revenue goals, and team structure. A qualified fractional CMO will conduct a brief audit before proposing an engagement scope. Look for someone with at least 10 years of senior marketing experience, a track record of driving pipeline growth, and specific experience in your industry.
