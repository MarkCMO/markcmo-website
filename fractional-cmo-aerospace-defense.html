The Aerospace and Defense Marketing Challenge

Aerospace and defense marketing is unique: government business development runs on relationships and contracting vehicles, not traditional marketing. But as defense primes diversify into commercial markets and commercial tech companies pursue defense contracts, the rules are changing. A fractional CMO who has worked in A&D understands how to build both commercial demand generation and government BD support.

Aerospace and Defense Marketing: Long Sales Cycles, Security Constraints, and Government Procurement

Aerospace and defense marketing is shaped by constraints that do not exist in commercial markets: ITAR regulations, security classification levels, procurement rules that prevent conventional digital advertising, and evaluation processes that take months or years rather than weeks. The fractional CMO for an A&D company builds the commercial strategy within these constraints -- focusing on the content, channels, and relationship strategies that are both effective and compliant with the regulatory environment.

Government contracting pursuit is a marketing problem as much as a business development problem. The companies that win consistently have built the relationships, the past performance record, and the qualification documentation before the RFP is issued -- because by the time the solicitation is public, the deck is largely set. The fractional CMO builds the pre-RFP marketing strategy: positioning the company correctly in the relevant agency's awareness, building relationships with program managers and contracting officers through industry events and technical conferences, and developing the capability statements and past performance narratives that will anchor the eventual proposal.

Commercial aerospace marketing follows more conventional B2B dynamics than defense, but with long sales cycles and a multi-year procurement timeline that requires a patient content strategy. The fractional CMO builds the content infrastructure that positions the company at each stage of the program lifecycle: early research and specification, competitive evaluation, source selection, and program execution. Technical credibility is the primary differentiator -- content that demonstrates domain expertise, specific performance data, and program execution capability is the most effective marketing investment in this category.

  1. Develop a government market positioning map -- identify the specific agencies, programs, and contracting vehicles where the company's capabilities align with the government's needs and build relationships in those areas before solicitations are issued
  2. Build a capability statement library -- short-form, NAICS-organized, and past-performance-anchored documents that can be customized for each opportunity and used in source selection conversations
  3. Establish an industry event presence strategy -- identify the two to three conferences or symposia where the target customers are present and build a consistent, expert-positioned attendance program
  4. Develop a technical thought leadership program -- white papers, conference presentations, and technical blog content that demonstrates domain expertise to program managers and contracting officers who evaluate companies before writing RFPs
  5. Implement a past performance documentation system -- ensure that every completed program generates a client testimonial, a performance metric summary, and a capability narrative that can be used in future competitive pursuits
  6. Map the teaming landscape -- identify the prime contractors, subcontractors, and small business partners that access your target programs and build a systematic teaming relationship program that generates subcontract opportunities

Aerospace and Defense Marketing: Commercial Strategy in a Compliance-Intensive Market

Aerospace and defense marketing operates in a procurement environment unlike any other B2B market. Government contracting cycles run 12-36 months, procurement is heavily regulated (FAR/DFARS for federal contracts), and the buying decision involves multiple government stakeholders, contracting officers, program managers, and technical evaluators whose alignment is necessary for contract award. Commercial space and defense marketing for prime contractors and Tier 1/2 suppliers requires understanding the government acquisition process as well as -- or better than -- conventional commercial marketing skills. The fractional CMO who serves A&D companies must bridge commercial marketing expertise with specific GovCon market knowledge.

The commercial priorities in A&D marketing divide between government business development and commercial segment marketing. On the government side, business development is the dominant commercial activity: identifying relevant programs, building relationships with program offices, participating in industry days and RFI processes, and positioning the company for upcoming contract opportunities. On the commercial side (commercial space, defense-adjacent technology companies, and commercial aviation), the marketing strategy resembles conventional B2B marketing more closely -- but with the specialized buyer language, technical credibility requirements, and long relationship cycles that characterize all A&D commercial relationships. The fractional CMO who serves A&D companies helps both tracks perform better without conflating the two.

Security and compliance requirements also shape A&D marketing in ways that constrain channels and content. Companies with facility clearances (Secret, Top Secret, or SCI) must not reveal classified relationships or capabilities in public marketing materials. Companies pursuing CMMC certification for DoD contracts must maintain specific cybersecurity standards in their marketing technology infrastructure. And companies selling into the defense supply chain must comply with ITAR (International Traffic in Arms Regulations) requirements that restrict certain marketing activities with foreign nationals. A fractional CMO who has not worked in A&D may not be aware of these constraints -- which is a meaningful selection criterion for companies in this market.

  1. Map the BD pipeline separately from commercial marketing metrics: government contract pursuits have different metrics (opportunities identified, teaming arrangements formed, proposals submitted, win rates) than commercial marketing metrics -- the fractional CMO builds reporting that covers both tracks with appropriate metrics for each
  2. Build the capability statement library: A&D buyers use capability statements to screen potential vendors before engaging in formal procurement processes -- having current, tailored capability statements for each major program office or commercial segment the company pursues is a basic business development requirement
  3. Establish PTAC (Procurement Technical Assistance Center) and SBA relationships if the company qualifies for set-aside programs: 8(a), SDVOSB, HUBZONE, and WOSB set-aside programs create procurement preferences that can accelerate government contract revenue, and the fractional CMO coordinates the certification maintenance and marketing strategy that leverages those certifications
  4. Build a teaming strategy: most A&D contracts require prime-sub relationships where the company is either the prime with subcontractors or a subcontractor to a prime -- identifying and cultivating the teaming relationships that increase competitive win rates is a critical business development function that the fractional CMO coordinates with BD leadership
  5. Develop a cleared facility marketing program if applicable: marketing to other cleared facilities for classified program support requires specific outreach approaches (industry associations, classified program databases, cleared-contractor networking events) that differ from conventional commercial marketing
  6. Create technical credibility content that addresses the A&D buyer's evaluation criteria: white papers on relevant technical domains, presentations at industry conferences (AUSA, AOC, AIAA, Space Symposium), and contributions to defense trade publications build the technical authority that government and commercial A&D buyers use to evaluate vendor credibility
