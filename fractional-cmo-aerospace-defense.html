The Aerospace and Defense Marketing Challenge

Aerospace and defense marketing is unique: government business development runs on relationships and contracting vehicles, not traditional marketing. But as defense primes diversify into commercial markets and commercial tech companies pursue defense contracts, the rules are changing. A fractional CMO who has worked in A&D understands how to build both commercial demand generation and government BD support.

Who You're Marketing To in Aerospace and Defense

The primary buyers in Aerospace and Defense: DoD program offices, prime contractors, OEM procurement, commercial aviation buyers, space program offices. Each of these decision-makers evaluates vendors differently and responds to different proof points. A Aerospace and Defense fractional CMO understands the buying committee dynamics and builds messaging that resonates with each stakeholder at the right stage of the decision process.

Channels That Work in Aerospace and Defense Marketing

The most effective marketing channels for Aerospace and Defense companies: conference and trade show presence (AIA, MRO, AUSA, Space Symposium), thought leadership and technical content, government BD support, commercial demand generation, LinkedIn for A&D networks. Channel selection must match where your specific buyers spend attention - not where your competitor is spending budget.

Aerospace and Defense Marketing: Long Sales Cycles, Security Constraints, and Government Procurement

Aerospace and defense marketing is shaped by constraints that do not exist in commercial markets: ITAR regulations, security classification levels, procurement rules that prevent conventional digital advertising, and evaluation processes that take months or years rather than weeks. The fractional CMO for an A&D company builds the commercial strategy within these constraints -- focusing on the content, channels, and relationship strategies that are both effective and compliant with the regulatory environment.

Government contracting pursuit is a marketing problem as much as a business development problem. The companies that win consistently have built the relationships, the past performance record, and the qualification documentation before the RFP is issued -- because by the time the solicitation is public, the deck is largely set. The fractional CMO builds the pre-RFP marketing strategy: positioning the company correctly in the relevant agency's awareness, building relationships with program managers and contracting officers through industry events and technical conferences, and developing the capability statements and past performance narratives that will anchor the eventual proposal.

Commercial aerospace marketing follows more conventional B2B dynamics than defense, but with long sales cycles and a multi-year procurement timeline that requires a patient content strategy. The fractional CMO builds the content infrastructure that positions the company at each stage of the program lifecycle: early research and specification, competitive evaluation, source selection, and program execution. Technical credibility is the primary differentiator -- content that demonstrates domain expertise, specific performance data, and program execution capability is the most effective marketing investment in this category.

  1. Develop a government market positioning map -- identify the specific agencies, programs, and contracting vehicles where the company's capabilities align with the government's needs and build relationships in those areas before solicitations are issued
  2. Build a capability statement library -- short-form, NAICS-organized, and past-performance-anchored documents that can be customized for each opportunity and used in source selection conversations
  3. Establish an industry event presence strategy -- identify the two to three conferences or symposia where the target customers are present and build a consistent, expert-positioned attendance program
  4. Develop a technical thought leadership program -- white papers, conference presentations, and technical blog content that demonstrates domain expertise to program managers and contracting officers who evaluate companies before writing RFPs
  5. Implement a past performance documentation system -- ensure that every completed program generates a client testimonial, a performance metric summary, and a capability narrative that can be used in future competitive pursuits
  6. Map the teaming landscape -- identify the prime contractors, subcontractors, and small business partners that access your target programs and build a systematic teaming relationship program that generates subcontract opportunities
