AMZ-AUD-001 · Forensic Digital Footprint Audit

Six findings on why a 20-year-old domain underperforms its age.

Every finding below was pulled directly from your live site. No assumptions, no generic checklist. Each one shows the current state with the evidence, the fix, the risk of leaving it, and my recommendation.

The Frame

Three Gates Decide Every Ranking

Before the findings, the lens I use. Ranking is not one thing you win or lose. It is three gates a page has to clear, in order. Amzur is leaking at all three, worst at the first and the third.

01

Can it be trusted technically?

Can Google and AI crawlers load, render, and trust the page fast enough to count it. This is where Amzur leaks the most.

02

Does it deserve the click?

Does the page clearly answer the intent behind the search. Mixed: deep service pages are strong, the homepage and architecture are not.

03

Does the domain have the weight?

Enough authority, citations, and third-party mentions to outrank incumbents and be picked by AI engines. The second-biggest leak.

The Findings

What The Live Site Actually Shows

1

Technical delivery is the single biggest drag

Critical · Gate 1
Current State

The homepage loads dozens of separate stylesheets and scripts from Elementor and its add-ons, and it is served from a single origin server with no CDN and no caching headers. The page is heavy before a single image loads.

Evidence pulled from amzur.com Homepage external CSS files: 47
Homepage script tags: 73
Raw HTML payload: ~377 KB (before images)
Server: Apache/2.4.41 (Ubuntu), no CDN detected
Cache-Control header: none returned
The Fix

Put the site behind a CDN such as Cloudflare, turn on edge caching and Brotli compression, and consolidate and defer the CSS and JavaScript. Remove unused Elementor add-ons that ship their own stylesheets.

Risk If Ignored

Core Web Vitals almost certainly fail on mobile, which is both a direct ranking factor and a conversion killer. Every organic visit and every paid click lands on a slow page, so you are paying for traffic that bounces. Global buyers on the UAE, ANZ, and Africa pages get the worst of it with no CDN in front of them.

My Recommendation

This is the highest-leverage, lowest-drama fix in the audit. A CDN plus a render-path cleanup can move mobile load from poor to good in days, and it lifts every other page on the site at once. I want a live Core Web Vitals field-data pull to put exact numbers on it, which is part of the access I requested.

2

The homepage throws away its most valuable on-page signal

High · Gate 2
Current State

The homepage uses four H1 tags, and every one of them is the brand slogan, not a keyword. The single most important on-page element points at zero commercial intent, and having four H1s breaks the page's semantic structure.

Evidence pulled from amzur.com H1 tags on homepage: 4 (should be 1)
H1 text: "Accelerate the SHIFT" (brand slogan, no keyword)
Title tag: good ("Accelerate Digital Transformation with AI & ERP | Amzur")
The Fix

One H1 that names what you sell and who it is for, for example "AI, ERP, and Cloud Services for Mid-Market Operations." Demote the slogan to a styled headline so it stays on brand without spending your strongest on-page signal.

Risk If Ignored

Your strongest page by authority tells Google nothing about what to rank it for. You are spending your best on-page real estate on a tagline that no buyer searches.

My Recommendation

Easy win, done in week one. Brand slogans belong in the visual design, never in the H1.

3

Deep service pages are strong, which is the good news

Strength · Gate 2
Current State

The NetSuite Implementation page has a clean title, a single H1, real depth, and valid FAQ schema. Rank Math is installed and even generating an llms.txt. This is not a team that cannot execute on-page. The skill is there, it is just applied unevenly across the site.

Evidence: amzur.com/erp/netsuite/netsuite-implementation-services/ H1 tags: 1 (correct) · H2 sections: 22
FAQ schema: present (FAQPage + 4 Questions)
Title: "NetSuite Implementation Services | Amzur ERP Experts"
The Fix

Standardize what the NetSuite page already does well across every money page, then build internal links from the blog into them so authority flows where it converts.

Risk If Ignored

Your best work stays isolated on a few pages instead of compounding into topical authority across the whole site.

My Recommendation

Do not rebuild, replicate. The winning pattern already exists in-house. This makes the rest of the engagement faster and cheaper.

4

The URL architecture is fractured, and it is the refocus scar

High · Gate 2 + 3
Current State

The sitemap mixes two URL trees for the same concept: most industry pages sit under /industry/, but at least one sits under /industries/. Same idea, two paths. That splits topical authority and signals a past migration that was never cleaned up, which lines up exactly with the refocus you mentioned hurting your SEO.

Evidence: page-sitemap.xml /industry/retail, /industry/manufacturing, /industry/insurance,
/industry/healthcare, /industry/energy-utilities
but: /industries/government-services  (split path)
The Fix

Pick one path, 301-redirect the orphans into it, consolidate the cluster, and fix the internal links to match. Audit for any other old refocus URLs leaking equity the same way.

Risk If Ignored

Google sees two half-built industries sections instead of one authoritative one, so neither ranks to its potential. This is precisely how a business refocus quietly bleeds the equity a 20-year-old domain should be compounding.

My Recommendation

A redirect-and-consolidation pass. Unglamorous, high return, and it directly addresses the thing you already suspect went wrong.

5

Technical hygiene gaps that are quick to close

Medium · Gate 1
Current State

The robots.txt sitemap directive is malformed with a trailing slash, which can impair sitemap discovery. Schema coverage is solid on the homepage (Organization, LocalBusiness, Service) but thin or missing on most interior pages.

Evidence: robots.txt Sitemap: https://amzur.com/sitemap_index.xml/  (trailing slash, should be none)
The Fix

Correct the robots directive and roll out Service, Breadcrumb, and Organization schema sitewide so every page is machine-readable.

Risk If Ignored

Minor individually, but together they signal a site that is not actively maintained, and small crawl and parse issues compound over hundreds of pages.

My Recommendation

Batch these into the first technical sprint. Low effort, and they remove friction from everything else.

6

Invisible in AI answer engines, the gap that should worry you most

Critical · Gate 3
Current State

I asked an AI answer engine the exact question your buyer asks it, and Amzur did not appear at all. The reason is structural: AI engines do not read your marketing. They assemble answers from third-party sources, the directories, review platforms, and listicles. Amzur's footprint is almost entirely first-party, so there is nothing external for an AI to cite, and it names the firms that are listed instead.

Query tested: "Best NetSuite implementation partners for mid-market manufacturing and distribution in the US?"
The answer engine named, with citations:
RSM USBryant Park ConsultingConcentrusEide BaillyMyers-HolumSikich
Amzur was not mentioned. Not in the list, not as an honorable mention.
Then I searched Amzur's own name.
The only results were Amzur's own website pages plus a single directory listing. The engine itself flagged that the results were "primarily from Amzur's own website and case studies, rather than independent third-party reviews." That one sentence is the whole diagnosis.
The llms.txt red herring

Amzur has an llms.txt file (Rank Math generated it). It does nothing today. ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews do not use llms.txt for retrieval or ranking. It is a checkbox that creates a false sense of being covered. Real AI visibility comes from third-party corroboration, not a file on the server.

The Fix

A Generative Engine Optimization push. Get Amzur placed and reviewed on the exact sources AI engines pull from (G2, Clutch, Goodfirms, ERP Research, SoftwareConnect, the official Oracle NetSuite partner directory), earn mentions in the top-partners listicles that already rank, and restructure key pages so answer engines can lift clean, attributable claims.

Risk If Ignored

This is the one that should scare you. Buyer research is moving into AI answers now, and that shift favors whoever the model already knows. Every month Amzur stays uncited, competitors with directory and review presence get recommended by default to buyers who never run a Google search at all. Classic SEO is a slow bleed. AI invisibility is future pipeline walking to a competitor before you are ever in the consideration set.

My Recommendation

Pair this with the technical fixes as the two headline moves of the engagement. The SEO floor makes your pages competitive. The GEO push makes you exist in the channel buyers are migrating to. It is also fast and visible: getting onto three or four authoritative sources and earning a couple of listicle placements can change what the AI says within a quarter. That is a result you can screenshot and show the board. Here is what the AI said in week one. Here is what it says now.

Still To Be Quantified

Two findings need your data to size

Two of the items above are diagnosed from strong proxies and need real data to put exact numbers on, which is exactly what my information request covers:

Authority and backlinks (Ahrefs or Semrush): to show the precise gap versus the firms outranking you and to map the winnable terms.

Core Web Vitals field data and Search Console: page 2 is where the fastest revenue lives, the queries where you already sit just below the fold, ready to be pushed up. I cannot see that without Search Console access.

The Reframe

Not Number One Everywhere. Number One Where It Pays.

The goal is not to rank first for everything. It is to fix the technical floor so every page can compete, consolidate the architecture so authority compounds instead of splits, then concentrate firepower on the 15 to 30 high-intent terms where you can realistically win and that actually convert to pipeline, while building the third-party presence that gets you cited in AI answers. That is a 90-day technical and architecture phase, then an ongoing authority and AI-visibility phase. The Scope of Work lays out exactly how.

The Bigger Problem

These six findings are symptoms, not the disease

Fixing them matters, but step back and the pattern is clear: a capable team is executing tactically with no one above them owning the strategy or the push to market. That is why the homepage targets a slogan, why the architecture fractured, why you are invisible in AI, and why the channels underperform.
