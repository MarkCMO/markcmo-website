If the retainer pricing is a stretch right now, we can flip the model: a smaller fixed cost, and MarkCMO earns a share of the revenue from the jobs the program actually books. We win when ROC wins.

The trade-off, stated plainly

Lower Risk Upfront, Shared Upside

The retainer model is the cheapest way to run this when it works, because ROC keeps 100% of the job revenue. The performance model is the safest way to start, because ROC pays little or nothing until the phone rings. The honest trade: revenue share costs more in total once the leads are flowing, because MarkCMO is taking on the cost and risk and sharing the upside. Pick it if cash flow today is the constraint, not because it is cheaper over a good year.

Hybrid Performance

A small base keeps the lights on; the rest is tied to closed jobs. ROC still covers the ad budget, so the performance fee stays modest. We win when ROC wins.
  • $2,500 / mo reduced management base
  • Marketing fuel at cost, paid by ROC (roughly $4,000 to $5,000 / mo for the 20-30 lead target)
  • 7% of collected revenue on program-sourced jobs
  • $3,000 reduced Foundation Build (or rolled into month one)
  • 12-month term
Best when: upfront cash is the only constraint and ROC wants to share upside instead of paying the full retainer on day one.

What Counts As A Program Job

Revenue share only applies to new business the program actually generated, tracked transparently so there is never a dispute. ROC's existing pipeline is never touched.

Counts toward the share

  • Calls through the tracked phone numbers we install
  • Local Service Ads and Google / Meta ad leads
  • Form fills from the landing pages we build
  • Google Business Profile calls and messages from profiles we manage
  • Only when the job is signed and collected

Excluded from the share

  • Word-of-mouth and personal referrals
  • Repeat customers and past clients
  • Builder, GC, or insurance-adjuster relationships ROC already has
  • Any lead ROC can show predates the program
  • Cancelled or uncollected jobs

Every attributed job appears in the shared monthly dashboard with its source. The performance fee is invoiced monthly on collected revenue (after ROC is paid), never on signed-but-unpaid contracts. Either party can audit the attribution log at any time.

Either way, the engine is the same
