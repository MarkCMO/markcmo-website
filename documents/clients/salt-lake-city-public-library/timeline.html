SLC-DA-003 · Phasing & Lead Times

We Move At Your Pace.

Engagement durations are not pre-committed. Each phase has a fixed scope and fixed fee, but the calendar is set together at kickoff and matched to the library's seasonal load, board cadence, and staff availability. This document covers the sequence of work, the hard external lead times we don't control, and what causes phases to take longer or shorter.

Discovery & Alignment Sequence

Five sequenced steps. Each one starts when its inputs are in hand. The library sets the calendar; we move the work along that calendar without rushing it.

Light inventory of systems in use plus a map of how technology decisions get made today. Built in parallel with interviews.

Operational Assessment Sequence

Phase 1 is presented for context. It is a separate fixed-fee engagement with its own contract. The library may or may not initiate Phase 1; there is no obligation either way and no expiration on the option.

01

Stakeholder mobilization

Full interview schedule built around the library's calendar. Read-only access to inventory deepens. Branch and program staff outreach begins.

Interview wave

Leadership re-interviewed deeper. Department heads, branch managers, programming staff, IT, finance, HR. Process maps drafted as interviews complete. Cadence set by the library.

Cross-team workflow maps completed. Technology decision rights documented (RACI). Service-delivery models inventoried.

Working session(s) with library leadership. 3-year future-state vision drafted. Priority initiatives identified. Phase 2 options scoped.

06

Final readout + Phase 2 proposal

Board-ready presentation delivered. Full Phase 1 deliverable handed over (3 reports, 1 presentation, 1 future-state document). Phase 2 proposal issued separately.

What Adjusts the Calendar

Things That Take Longer Or Shorter.

Library-side variables (we follow your lead)

Stakeholder availability. Discovery and Phase 1 are interview-driven. Branch staff, programming staff, board liaisons, and seasonal leadership absences set the realistic cadence. The library tells us what's workable; we sequence around it.

Library-side variables (continued)

Seasonal calendar. Summer programming, school-break events, fundraising drives, board-meeting cycles, end-of-fiscal-year cadences. We don't run interview waves through your peak periods unless you specifically want us to.

External variables (neither side controls)

Procurement & board approvals. The library's contract execution path, board approval cadence, and any external review processes set the floor on time-to-kickoff. We don't accelerate these — we wait for them.

Quality bar (we hold the line on this)

The deliverable. If the library wants to compress a phase aggressively, we issue a written change order before the work starts — never on the back end. The fixed fee covers a thorough engagement; faster work that risks the deliverable's quality is the only thing we say no to.
