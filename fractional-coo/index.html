What Does a Fractional COO Do?

A fractional Chief Operating Officer takes ownership of how a company runs - its systems, its processes, its team structure, and its operational performance - without being a full-time employee. The role is operational accountability, not advisory. A fractional COO identifies what is slowing the company down, builds the systems that replace the chaos, and holds the leadership team accountable to the metrics that matter.

The day-to-day work of a fractional COO depends entirely on what is broken. In a company that has grown fast and outrun its systems, the first priority is usually process documentation, role clarity, and building the operating cadence that keeps teams moving without requiring the CEO to be in every conversation. In a company preparing for acquisition or PE due diligence, the focus shifts to making operations legible: SOPs, org charts, financial reporting, and the documentation that gives a buyer or investor confidence in the business they are evaluating.

In every engagement, a fractional COO is responsible for:

  • Operations strategy and roadmap - diagnosing the operational constraints that are slowing revenue growth and building a plan to eliminate them
  • Process design and documentation - replacing tribal knowledge and heroic individual effort with documented, repeatable processes the team can run without constant oversight
  • Team structure and org design - clarifying roles, responsibilities, and reporting lines so the right work goes to the right people and accountability is clear
  • KPI and performance accountability - building the measurement infrastructure that shows leaders what is actually happening in the business, weekly and monthly
  • Systems and technology architecture - selecting, implementing, and integrating the operational software that makes the company scalable without adding headcount proportionally
  • Vendor and partner management - rationalizing external relationships and ensuring critical vendors are performing to the standards the business needs
  • Hiring and onboarding infrastructure - building the talent acquisition and onboarding processes that let companies scale without each new hire being a six-month recovery project
  • Exit and M&A preparation - making the business operationally ready for due diligence, acquisition, or growth financing by ensuring systems and documentation are in order

Who Needs a Fractional COO?

The fractional COO model solves a specific and common problem: companies that have grown fast enough to create operational chaos, but not large enough to justify a $200,000+ full-time COO. The symptoms are predictable: the CEO is still in every decision, the team is growing but performance is not, processes do not exist or are not followed, and every quarter feels harder than the last despite more people and more revenue.

You should consider hiring a fractional COO if any of these describe your business:

  • The CEO is still the operational center of the business - everything routes through them
  • You have scaled beyond 10 employees and operations feel chaotic or inconsistent
  • Revenue is growing but profitability is not following
  • You are preparing for PE investment, acquisition, or a growth financing round
  • You recently made acquisitions and need to integrate operations across entities
  • Your team is busy but results are not proportional to the effort
  • You do not have clear visibility into operational performance on a weekly basis
  • You are considering a business succession or exit and need operations documented and transferable

When NOT to Hire a Fractional COO

A fractional COO is not the right answer for every operational challenge, and the distinction matters.

If your business is under $1 million in revenue and still validating its model, operational infrastructure is premature. At that stage, you need to prove the business works before you build systems around it. Documented processes for a business that is still pivoting are largely wasted effort.

If the operational issues are really people issues - a specific employee who is not performing, a culture problem, or a team conflict - that is a leadership and HR challenge, not a systems challenge. A COO can build the structures around those situations but cannot substitute for the leadership decisions the CEO needs to make.

If what you need is someone executing operational tasks daily without strategic oversight, that is an operations manager role, not a COO role. A fractional COO designs the system and installs the thinking. The daily execution belongs with the team.

"Most operational problems are not process problems. They are clarity problems. The team does not know what they are supposed to do, how success is measured, or who owns each outcome. Once you establish those three things, most operational chaos resolves itself."

Fractional COO Services

A fractional COO engagement with Mark Gabrielli covers the full operational leadership function. The specific work active in any engagement depends on your company's stage, team, and priority constraints.

Operations Diagnostic and Roadmap

A structured audit of your current operational state: processes, systems, team, reporting, and performance. Delivers a prioritized roadmap that identifies the constraints costing the most revenue and team capacity.

Process Design and SOPs

Turning institutional knowledge into documented, repeatable standard operating procedures that work whether or not the founder or key employee is in the room. Built for scale, not complexity.

Organizational Design and Role Clarity

Restructuring the org chart so roles match the business you are building, not the one you built three years ago. Includes job descriptions, accountability frameworks, and reporting structures that support growth.

KPI Dashboards and Accountability Systems

Building the measurement infrastructure that gives leadership a weekly view of operational health: what is on track, what is behind, and where decisions are needed. Tied to outcomes, not activity.

Systems and Technology Stack

Selecting, implementing, and integrating the operational technology - project management, ERP, CRM, HRIS, and automation tooling - that replaces manual work and creates operational leverage without proportional headcount growth.

Team Hiring and Onboarding Infrastructure

Building the hiring process, interview frameworks, and onboarding systems that turn each new hire into a productive team member in weeks, not months. Includes job scoring, structured interviews, and 30/60/90 day onboarding plans.

Financial Operations and Reporting

Aligning operational performance with financial outcomes. Building the reporting cadence and operational model that connects headcount, process efficiency, and revenue in one coherent picture for the leadership team and board.

Exit and M&A Operational Readiness

Preparing operations for due diligence: documentation, system audits, process standardization, and the operational narrative that helps buyers understand and value what you have built. For full exit strategy, rollup, succession planning, and M&A advisory, see WETYR Corp.

How a Fractional COO Engagement Works

1

Free 30-Minute Diagnostic Call

The first conversation costs nothing and covers everything. We talk about your operational situation, where the biggest pain points are, and what the business needs to do to move to the next level. By the end of the call you will know whether a fractional COO engagement makes sense and what it would focus on.

2

Operations Diagnostic (Week 1-2)

A structured review of every major operational area: team, processes, systems, KPIs, vendor relationships, and reporting. Includes interviews with key team members to understand where work actually happens versus where it is supposed to happen. The diagnostic produces a clear picture of what is working, what is broken, and what is missing.

3

90-Day Operational Roadmap (Week 2-3)

A prioritized action plan that addresses the three to five highest-leverage operational changes the business can make in the next 90 days. Each item has an owner, a timeline, a success metric, and a clear connection to business outcomes. Not a list of nice-to-haves - a plan of what will move the needle most.

4

Execution and Accountability (Ongoing)

The fractional COO role is active, not advisory. That means running the weekly leadership operating cadence, holding team members accountable to commitments, reviewing performance against KPIs, and making the operational decisions that keep the business moving without requiring the CEO's involvement in everything.

Fractional COO - Frequently Asked Questions

What is a fractional COO?
A fractional COO is a senior Chief Operating Officer engaged on a part-time or retainer basis rather than as a full-time employee. The fractional COO takes ownership of operational strategy and execution, manages operational performance, and is accountable to efficiency and scale outcomes - the same responsibilities as an in-house COO, at a fraction of the cost. Retainer engagements typically range from $8,000 to $20,000 per month.
How much does a fractional COO cost?
A fractional COO typically costs between $8,000 and $20,000 per month on a retainer basis. Compare this to a full-time COO, which averages $180,000 to $320,000 per year in base salary, plus benefits, equity, and a recruiting timeline of 3 to 5 months. Mark Gabrielli's engagements are month-to-month with no long-term contract required.
What is the difference between a fractional COO and an operations manager?
An operations manager executes and oversees day-to-day operational tasks. A fractional COO designs the operational architecture - the systems, structures, KPIs, and accountability frameworks - and holds leadership accountable to strategic operational outcomes. An operations manager runs the machine. A fractional COO builds and engineers the machine.
What size company needs a fractional COO?
The fractional COO model is best suited for companies between $1 million and $50 million in annual revenue with 5 to 150 employees. This is the growth stage where operational complexity has outpaced informal management, but a $250,000 full-time COO is not yet a justified hire. It is also an ideal model for companies in transition - post-acquisition, pre-exit, or in a leadership gap.
How quickly can a fractional COO start?
A fractional COO can typically start within days to two weeks of agreement. The operations diagnostic and first deliverables are usually completed within the first two to three weeks of engagement. This compares to a full-time COO search that averages 3 to 5 months from launch to start date.
How many hours per week does a fractional COO work?
Most fractional COO engagements run between 10 and 20 hours per week. The first 30 to 60 days are typically the most intensive, as the diagnostic, roadmap, and initial process work require more hands-on time. After the foundation is built, the engagement often shifts toward a lighter ongoing accountability role at 8 to 12 hours per week.
What results can I expect in the first 90 days?
In the first 90 days, a fractional COO engagement typically delivers: a completed operations audit, a documented 90-day roadmap, at least 3 to 5 key processes documented as SOPs, a KPI dashboard that gives leadership weekly visibility, and measurable improvement in at least one major operational constraint. Most companies see decision-making speed and team clarity improve within the first 30 days as roles and accountabilities are clarified.
Can a fractional COO also serve as a fractional CMO?
Mark Gabrielli serves as both a fractional COO and a fractional CMO, depending on company needs. Some companies need only operational leadership. Some need only marketing leadership. Many fast-growing companies need both tightened simultaneously - which is where the dual-role engagement delivers the most concentrated value. See the Fractional CMO page for full details on that service.
Can a fractional COO help prepare for M&A or business sale?
Yes. Operational readiness is one of the most significant value drivers in any M&A process. A fractional COO can document processes, build reporting infrastructure, rationalize the org chart, and create the operational narrative that gives buyers and investors confidence in the business. For full M&A advisory, exit strategy, rollup planning, and succession planning services, Mark leads WETYR Corporation.
Will a fractional COO manage our team?
Yes, within the scope of the engagement. A fractional COO typically runs the leadership operating cadence, holds department leads accountable to their commitments, and may directly manage operational staff depending on the engagement structure. Where a full-time operations team lead is needed, the fractional COO helps define the role and evaluate candidates.
What industries does Mark Gabrielli serve as a fractional COO?
Mark has fractional COO experience across B2B SaaS, healthcare operations, aerospace and defense, manufacturing, professional services, financial services, technology services, real estate operations, and consumer brands. He has particular experience in operationally complex businesses where the gap between what the founder envisioned and what the org can actually execute is the primary constraint on growth.
How is a fractional COO different from a business consultant?
A business consultant advises and delivers recommendations. A fractional COO executes and is accountable to outcomes. Consultants typically work on a defined project with a handoff at the end. A fractional COO is embedded in the business, attends leadership meetings, manages team performance, and is responsible for the operational results week over week - not just for the quality of the deck they produce.
Can a fractional COO work remotely?
Yes. Most fractional COO work is conducted remotely via video meetings, collaborative tools, and embedded communication with your team. Mark Gabrielli serves clients across all 50 states remotely, with in-person availability for critical workshops, offsites, and board meetings in certain markets.
How do I know if I need a fractional COO or a COO hire?
If the business is generating between $1M and $30M and has not had operational leadership, a fractional COO is almost always the right first move. It gets the systems built and the team structured without the cost and risk of a full-time hire. Once the operational infrastructure is in place and the company is ready to scale above $30M to $50M, hiring a full-time COO - sometimes the person who has been working alongside the fractional COO - becomes a cleaner and higher-probability decision.
