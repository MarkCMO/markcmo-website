The problem

A bathtub with the drain half-plugged

Between Sebastian Inlet and Ponce de Leon Inlet lies a 50+ mile stretch of barrier island with no open ocean connection, and Port Canaveral's only link to the Banana River is a navigation lock that throttles exchange. Water in north Brevard sits for 148 to 220+ days versus 2 to 16 days near Sebastian. Nutrients concentrate instead of washing out - and the result is algae blooms, fish kills, and lost seagrass.

The nitrogen driving those blooms is roughly 3.4 million pounds a year. The biggest sources are the legacy muck on the bottom (~35%) and sewage and septic (~26%) - not lawn fertilizer, which is barely 6%. That means the fixes that matter are not the obvious ones.

The approach

Rank every fix by cost per pound of nitrogen

When you score each intervention the way Brevard's own lagoon plan does - dollars per pound of nitrogen removed - the flashy projects fall to the bottom and the cheap, fast wins rise to the top. Stormwater baffle boxes and a handful of engineered canal "demonstration cells" clear visible water in a single season for single-digit millions. Oyster reef, muck dredging, and desalination are the bigger, slower builds that finish the job once the momentum and funding are in place.

The one-line answer

Biggest impact, lowest cost, shortest time is not the oyster mega-reef or the desalination plant. It is stormwater baffle boxes plus canal demonstration cells plus the cheapest plant upgrades - roughly $5M for visibly clear water in five neighborhoods inside 18 months, while removing real nitrogen at the lowest cost per pound available.

The ask

Fund the flagship, prove the model, scale what works

The proposal to the Board is simple: fund a ~$5M, 18-month flagship - 20 baffle boxes, 5 canal demonstration cells, one cost-effective plant upgrade, and lagoon-wide clam seeding - largely from grants Brevard already touches. It removes real nitrogen at the lowest cost per pound, and it puts visibly clear water in five neighborhoods before the next budget cycle. That visible win is what earns the funding for the oyster reef, muck dredging, and desalination utility that finish the job.
