Five findings that shape the plan

  • The lagoon is a bathtub with the drain half-plugged. A 50+ mile inlet-free barrier island and the Port Canaveral lock leave water sitting 148 to 220+ days in north Brevard versus 2 to 16 days near Sebastian.
  • Muck is the biggest nitrogen source, not fertilizer. Bottom muck ~35%, sewage and septic ~26%, lawn fertilizer barely 6%.
  • The fast, cheap, visible wins are not the mega-projects. Baffle boxes ($88/lb) and canal demonstration cells clear visible water in one season - oyster reef ($392/lb) and septic-to-sewer ($443-872/lb) cost 4 to 10x more per pound.
  • Oysters and clams are the scale play, desalination a water utility. Both help; neither is a fast cure, and desal must inject its nutrient brine deep underground.
  • Sequence wins. Cheap source control and demonstration cells first, then clams and cheap plant upgrades, then muck dredging, oyster reef, and desalination.

FDEP's adopted plan requires cutting 380,239 lbs nitrogen/yr lagoon-wide (a 45% reduction); Brevard's own share is a 64,372 lbs/yr cut. These are the numbers the plan is measured against.

Why fund this first

For a fraction of one desal train, this removes measurable nitrogen across every cheap lever at once and puts visibly clear water in five neighborhoods within 18 months - the political and philanthropic unlock for the $100M+ scale-up. Largely fundable through Save Our Indian River Lagoon, NOAA, NFWF, and SJRWMD programs Brevard already touches.
