  • The lagoon is a bathtub with the drain half-plugged. A 50+ mile inlet-free barrier island and the Port Canaveral lock mean water sits 148 to 220+ days in north Brevard versus 2 to 16 days near Sebastian. Poor flushing is the root cause.
  • Muck is the biggest nitrogen source, not fertilizer. Bottom muck is ~35%, sewage and septic ~26%, lawn fertilizer barely 6%.
  • Oysters must be sized by nitrogen removed, not the "50 gallons a day" headline. To offset the regulator-required cut you need ~850 to 2,840 acres of reef, at ~$43M to $344M.
  • Desalination is a water utility, not a lagoon cure. At 25 MGD it would take ~23 years to cycle the lagoon once, and it concentrates nutrients into a brine that must be injected deep underground.

The fertilizer myth

FAU Harbor Branch found that of the human-sourced nitrogen, roughly 79% is sewage and septic, only ~21% is fertilizer. The fertilizer ordinance helped (~46,000 lbs/yr cut), but muck and sewage are where the nitrogen actually is.

What "clean" legally requires

FDEP's adopted plan requires cutting 380,239 lbs N/yr lagoon-wide (a 45% reduction); Brevard's own share is a 64,372 lbs N/yr cut. These are the numbers the oyster system is sized against.

Sizing the oyster filter

The defensible method is remove-the-nitrogen, not filter-the-volume.
V = A × d = 160,000 ac × 4 ft = 640,000 acre-ft = ~208.5 billion gallons // lagoon volume

The headline sizing

To carry oysters' fair share of the regulator-required cut, build 850 to 2,840 acres of reef (~170 to 575 million oysters) at $43M to $344M. The animals are cheap - hatchery seed is only ~$225 to $2,000 per acre; the cost is the reef substrate and labor. Brevard's current 14.5-acre target is under 2% of what is needed.

04

The desalination reality check

A legitimate water utility with a nutrient side-benefit, and one hard physical limit.
Ncaptured = Q × C × rejection = 25 MGD × ~1 mg/L × 85% ≈ ~64,700 lbs N/yr

The optimistic read

~64,700 lbs/yr is almost exactly Brevard's entire TMDL reduction requirement. On paper a 25 MGD plant on lagoon water could shoulder the county's regulatory nitrogen cut as a bonus stacked on water-supply revenue.

The physical limit

That same figure is only ~2% of the 3.4M lb gross load, and at 25 MGD the plant needs ~23 years to cycle the lagoon once. You cannot pump a 208-billion-gallon lagoon clean.

The brine problem

Reverse osmosis does not destroy nutrients - it concentrates them into the reject brine. Returning that saltier, nutrient-richer stream to a poorly-flushed lagoon would make things worse and is a permitting non-starter. The brine must go down a Class I deep injection well. Budget it as core infrastructure, and pitch the plant as a drinking-water utility whose nutrient capture is a bonus.
