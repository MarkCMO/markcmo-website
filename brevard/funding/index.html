Not eligible: the RESTORE Act

RESTORE Act money flows only to the 23 Gulf-of-Mexico coastal counties (Deepwater Horizon penalties). Brevard is on the Atlantic coast and is not eligible. Do not build RESTORE, Triumph Gulf Coast, or the Gulf Consortium into the plan - the Atlantic-coast analog is the NOAA and NFWF federal programs plus the state, regional, and local sources above.

02

The partnering structure

Most restoration grants require a public or nonprofit applicant. Here is how a private venture sits inside that.
  • Grantee of record = a public or nonprofit partner. Best fits: Brevard County Natural Resources (the SOIRL administrator), a city, a university (UF/IFAS, Florida Tech, UCF), or - the proven vehicle for oyster/clam work - Brevard Zoo's Restore Our Shores.
  • The private venture = contractor, technology provider, or O&M operator under that grantee, or the host/landowner.
  • Own the pieces you can hold outright: a Water Quality Enhancement Area to monetize nutrient reduction as sellable credits (private-eligible since 2024), and the desalination utility (rate-recovery + AWS cost-share, tax-exempt bonds via a municipal partner).
  • Muck removal and stormwater are strongest as SOIRL Project Plan additions and SJRWMD cost-share projects, with your firm as the dredging or treatment contractor.

04

The 90-day launch

From decision to shovels and sensors in the water.
Weeks 1-3 · Stand it up

Form the partnership and lock the stack

Align Brevard County Natural Resources, Brevard Zoo Restore Our Shores, and a university; confirm the grant stack; select the 5 candidate canal cells and the worst stormwater outfalls with county GIS.

Weeks 3-8 · Baseline & order

Measure, order, and file

Start baseline monitoring (Secchi depth, nitrogen, phosphorus) on the candidate cells; order the first baffle boxes; file the FDEP Environmental Resource Permit and USACE authorizations.

Weeks 6-12 · Fund & contract

Submit grants, contract suppliers, go public

Submit the SOIRL project request and NFWF/NOAA pre-proposals; contract the hydraulic-dredge and clam-seed suppliers; hold a community kickoff to build the political and volunteer base.

05

Permitting & suppliers

Permitting pathway

  • FDEP Environmental Resource Permit (ch. 62-330) for in-water work - reefs, dredging, demonstration cells, and the WQEA.
  • US Army Corps of Engineers for dredge-and-fill and Port Canaveral lock coordination.
  • SJRWMD for cost-share alignment and lagoon-basin review.
  • FDEP UIC Class I permit for a desalination injection well (multi-year - start early).

Delivery partners

  • Bivalves & seagrass: Brevard Zoo Restore Our Shores, UF Whitney Lab, UF Center for Coastal Solutions, Sea & Shoreline; hatcheries (Horn Point/ORP, Ward, Hoopers Island).
  • Stormwater: nutrient-separating baffle boxes (Oldcastle) with Bold & Gold / BAM media.
  • Dredging: hydraulic-dredge contractors + geotube dewatering.
  • Desal EPC: Carollo, Black & Veatch, Hazen, Kimley-Horn; membranes (DuPont, Toray, Hydranautics); energy recovery (Energy Recovery Inc.).
The move

Fund the $5M flagship largely from grants Brevard already touches, run it through a public or nonprofit partner with the venture as operator, and own the two pieces that can be private - the nutrient-credit area and the desalination utility. Prove visible clear water in 18 months, and the $100M+ scale-up funds itself.
