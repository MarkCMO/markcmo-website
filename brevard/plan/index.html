The action plan

Optimizing for the most nitrogen removed per dollar in the least time. The surprising result: the oyster mega-reef and the desalination plant are not the quick wins.

What the ranking tells you

Baffle boxes ($88/lb) win on both axes - cheapest and installable in weeks. Cheap plant upgrades ($125/lb, 11,000+ lbs each) are the best big-block buys. Oyster reef ($392) and septic-to-sewer ($443 to $872) are 4 to 10x more expensive per pound - worth doing at scale later, wrong place to start.

Two footnotes: muck dredging looks mid-pack per annual pound but removed legacy nitrogen at only ~$10/lb at Eau Gallie, and it deletes 35% of the load permanently. And clams rewrite the bivalve math - seed is ~$6,000 per million and a drone covers an acre in 15 minutes, versus oyster reef at $392/lb.

02

The three-layer strategy

Stop new nitrogen cheaply, prove a visible win fast, then buy the biggest cheap blocks.
Layer 1 · 0-6 mo

Turn off the cheapest taps

Baffle boxes at the worst outfalls plus fertilizer enforcement. Cheapest dollars per pound, installed in weeks, no science risk.

Layer 2 · 6-18 mo

Win visibly in the canals

Five demonstration cells: vacuum muck, seed clams, hang oysters, float wetlands, aerate. Water clears in one season - the proof that unlocks funding.

Layer 3 · 1-3 yr

Buy the biggest cheap blocks

Cheap plant upgrades (~11,000 lbs each at $125/lb) and muck removal in the worst hotspots. Large permanent removals at low unit cost.

Why not lead with oysters or desal

A well-flushed open lagoon will not show a fast visible clarity change from bivalves at any achievable density - that is a measured finding. So oysters and clams pay off slowly and at scale, and desalination is a 5-to-7-year build. Leading with baffle boxes + demonstration cells + cheap plant upgrades delivers measurable nitrogen cuts and visible clear water inside 18 months, for single-digit millions.

The standard structure: a public or nonprofit partner (Brevard County, a city, a university, or Brevard Zoo's Restore Our Shores) is the grantee of record; the private venture is the contractor, technology provider, or operator, and owns the pieces it can hold outright - the nutrient-credit area and the desalination utility. Note: the RESTORE Act does not apply - that is Gulf-coast-only money; Brevard is Atlantic.

The honest verdict

Oysters are the best living answer at scale, and desalination is a real self-funding water utility - but neither works on dirty, stagnant water. Muck removal and sewage control come first (61% of the nitrogen), the baffle boxes and demonstration cells deliver the fast visible win, and the big permanent builds follow the proof. Fund the flagship, and the rest follows.
