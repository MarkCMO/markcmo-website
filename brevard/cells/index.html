The five demonstration cells

The flagship lives or dies on visible proof. This is how the five canal cells are chosen, which Brevard reaches are the candidates, and exactly how we measure that the water got clearer - with a control to prove we caused it.

01

How we pick a cell

The ideal cell is the worst offender - small, stagnant, and nutrient-choked - because that is where filtering visibly wins fastest.

02

Candidate reaches in Brevard

County-known canal and tributary muck sites that already fit the profile - final five confirmed with county GIS and field checks.
  • Venetian / dead-end residential canals (North IRL and Banana River) - classic small, stagnant, mucky canals already in the county dredge inventory.
  • Sykes Creek and the Grand Canal (Merritt Island / Satellite Beach) - poorly flushed, muck-laden, dense residential docks.
  • Turkey Creek and Crane Creek mouths (Palm Bay / Melbourne) - tributary muck sinks; Turkey Creek was the original proof-of-concept dredge site.
  • Banana River finger canals - among the worst-flushed water in the whole system (the Port Canaveral lock starves them of tidal relief).

The clearance math, per cell

A typical 1,500 ft canal holds ~5.4M gallons and flushes in ~14 days, so it needs ~385,000 gallons cleared per day. One drone drop of clam seed across the floor plus oysters on the docks clears several times that - so the water measurably clears within 12 to 18 months. See the field guide for the full stack (muck vacuum + clams + oyster mats + floating wetland + aeration).

The control is the proof

Every treated cell is paired with a matched untreated control cell monitored identically. Clarity that improves in the treated cell but not the control is the difference between a compelling before-and-after photo and a defensible, fundable result. This is also what certifies the nitrogen reduction for sellable nutrient credits.

04

What a win unlocks

Five cells, ~$1.0 to $1.4M, visible clearer water in ~18 months, each with a control proving the cause. That result does three things at once:

  • Proves the model - a real before-and-after in five Brevard neighborhoods, not a projection.
  • Unlocks the capital - the political and philanthropic green light for the $100M+ oyster reef, muck dredging, and desalination scale-up.
  • Generates revenue - certified nitrogen reductions become sellable credits, and the method becomes a repeatable, fundable product for every dead-end canal in the lagoon.
The whole plan in one sentence

Start where the water is worst and smallest, filter it clear fast and cheap, prove it against a control, and let that visible win pay for the big permanent builds that finish the lagoon.
